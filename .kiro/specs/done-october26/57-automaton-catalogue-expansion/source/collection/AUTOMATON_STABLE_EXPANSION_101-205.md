Work down the list in numerical order. Each number identifies one stable proposal. Names, strategies, and arena associations are game design proposals. The eight Squadron entries contain complete planning blueprints for 48 robots and 40 teams, including per-robot refinement sequences and budgets. The other 97 entries have concept-level game plans; their portrait rosters and selected source editions are recorded inline and in the production records. ## #101 — Geonosis Droid Foundry

**Franchise:** Star Wars  
**Strategy:** Swarm · **Target roster:** 11 robots  
**Catalogue key:** `geonosis-droid-foundry`

**Proposed robots:** Kalani; B1 battle droid; B2 super battle droid; Droideka; BX commando droid; IG-100 MagnaGuard; T-series tactical droid; B1 commander; dwarf spider droid; LM-432 crab droid; Octuptarra droid.

**Identity:** a surviving Separatist production line repurposes its machines for the arena. The stable expands through recognizable classes rather than eleven recoloured infantry portraits.

**Strategy personality:** prioritize roster growth and practical equipment. Light infantry, ranged platforms, and close-combat specialists give the army different build focuses within the existing Swarm strategy. Kalani supplies the stable's narrative leadership; he does not grant a new command buff.

The numbered names are original arena unit labels for source machine classes. They are not presented as named Star Wars characters. Only the two B1 entries deliberately share their basic chassis; the eleven portraits cover ten distinct chassis families.

**Visual family:** source-specific finishes, scuffed industrial metal, visible mechanisms, and subtle Geonosian factory atmosphere. Preserve the B1's thin geometry, the B2's broad mass, and the walker silhouettes. Shared lighting should unify the collection without forcing every machine into the same colour palette.

**Source edition:** use the established prequel/Clone Wars machine designs, with Kalani based on his Star Wars Rebels appearance. This is an era-spanning Separatist arena collection, not a claim that these exact units fought together in one battle.

## #102 — Rebel Droid Partnership

**Franchise:** Star Wars  
**Strategy:** Duo · **Target roster:** 2 robots  
**Catalogue key:** `rebel-droid-partnership`

**Proposed robots:** R2-D2 and C-3PO.

**Identity:** the galaxy's most familiar droid partnership relies on survival, resourcefulness, and complementary investments.

**Strategy personality:** a compact mobile R2-D2 build beside a defensive, tactical C-3PO build. Concentrate upgrades in the pair. These are proposed arena roles; C-3PO remains a protocol droid in the source, so this stable has a stronger adaptation requirement than the battle-droid army.

**Visual direction:** original-trilogy appearances. R2-D2 keeps his blue-and-white cylindrical body and silver dome. C-3PO keeps his gold protocol-droid body, exposed midriff wiring, and original film details. Use realistic surface wear and the shared studio lighting while retaining their familiar construction. Added combat equipment should remain visually subordinate to their identities.

References: [R2-D2](https://www.starwars.com/databank/r2-d2), [C-3PO](https://www.starwars.com/databank/c-3po/), [companion relationship](https://www.starwars.com/databank/abafar).

## #103 — Droid Bounty Syndicate

**Franchise:** Star Wars  
**Strategy:** Trio · **Target roster:** 3 robots  
**Catalogue key:** `droid-bounty-syndicate`

**Proposed robots:** IG-88, IG-11, and 4-LOM.

**Identity:** three independent mechanical hunters form an arena collection around precision, relentless pressure, and paid contracts. Their association is proposed for Armoured Souls, not a canonical bounty-hunting team.

**Strategy personality:** IG-88 as the precise ranged specialist, IG-11 as the aggressive mobile gunfighter, and 4-LOM as the durable, calculating counterpart. The three builds grow together under Trio's existing purchasing and tuning rules.

**Visual direction:** IG-88 uses his battered original-trilogy chrome body; IG-11 uses his first-season Mandalorian bounty-hunter form; 4-LOM keeps the rusty protocol-droid body and insect-like head seen in The Empire Strikes Back. Different poses and signature weapon arrangements must distinguish the two IG-series robots. IG-11's picture should depict the mechanical hunter himself rather than an IG-12 configuration carrying Grogu.

References: [IG-88](https://www.starwars.com/databank/ig-88), [IG-11](https://www.starwars.com/databank/ig-11), [4-LOM](https://www.starwars.com/databank/4-LOM).

## #104 — Grievous War Forge

**Franchise:** Star Wars  
**Strategy:** Paragon · **Target roster:** 1 robot  
**Catalogue key:** `grievous-war-forge`

**Proposed robots:** General Grievous.

**Identity:** one fearsome cyborg warlord receives the stable's concentrated investment.

**Strategy personality:** an aggressive close-combat flagship with a clear premium equipment and refinement plan. His source-story strength does not set his Armoured Souls attributes or bypass normal budgets.

**Visual direction:** Revenge of the Sith design, recognizable pale armour, skeletal construction, hunched posture, and organic eyes. Use an open four-arm combat pose with the signature lightsabers visible and enough space around each blade for thumbnail readability. A cape may be omitted in the combat pose to keep the limbs clear.

Grievous is a cyborg, which fits the catalogue's existing convention that includes RoboCop. His four-arm artwork does not create four independently equipped weapon slots: the eventual blueprint must map him to a supported loadout, with the source-faithful portrait taking priority over literal equipment-slot illustration.

References: [General Grievous](https://www.starwars.com/databank/general-grievous), [official description of Grievous as a cyborg warlord](https://www.starwars.com/databank/Count-Dooku).

## #105 — Rebel Droid Network

**Strategy:** Generalist · **Target roster:** 3 robots  
**Catalogue key:** `rebel-droid-network`

**Proposed robots:** Chopper (C1-10P), AP-5, and K-2SO.

**Identity:** a resourceful collection of Rebel-aligned droids combines a patched astromech, a former inventory droid, and a reprogrammed security machine. This is a themed arena collection rather than an assertion that all three operated as a canonical squad.

## #106 — Jawa Salvage Exchange

**Franchise:** Star Wars  
**Strategy:** Tycoon · **Target roster:** 3 robots  
**Catalogue key:** `jawa-salvage-exchange`

**Proposed robots:** R5-D4, WED Treadwell 02, and GNK Power Droid 03.

**Identity:** a small salvage enterprise turns battered utility hardware into an arena business. Jawas provide the stable's setting and management flavour; the robot roster consists of the droids.

**Strategy personality:** prioritize the Tycoon strategy's income facilities, then invest in a modest, repaired roster. R5-D4 is the mobile general-purpose machine, the Treadwell is the precise tool-using specialist, and the GNK provides the sturdy defensive silhouette. These are arena interpretations of civilian machines, not claims that they are canonical combatants.

**Visual direction:** classic Tatooine service hardware. R5-D4 has his recognizable red-and-white flat-topped astromech head; the Treadwell retains its tracked base, thin neck, and tool arms; the GNK retains its box-shaped walking-generator body. Use sand, oxidized metal, patched housings, and restrained grime. Avoid turning the tiny GNK into an unrelated humanoid mech or equipping the Treadwell with a different source model's anatomy.

References: [R5-D4](https://www.starwars.com/databank/r5-d4), [WED Treadwell droid](https://www.starwars.com/databank/wed-treadwell-droid), [gonk power droids](https://www.starwars.com/databank/gonk-droid).

## Shared roster and art direction

### Roster rules

1. **Recognizable machines first.** A portrait should identify its theme before the player reads the name. Distinct walkers, service robots, mechanical creatures, and clockwork bodies add more variety than another similar armoured humanoid.
2. **Use one stable per newly introduced property by default.** Expand an existing franchise when factions or roster identities justify distinct stables. The original Autobot/Decepticon split already demonstrates this approach; the numbered Star Wars stables extend it deliberately.
3. **Match the strategy to the roster.** Target counts follow the newer starter catalogue: **Paragon 1, Duo 2, Trio 3, Generalist 3, Tycoon 3, Swarm 11**; the new **Squadron** proposal adds **3 opening → 6 maximum**. Use supported reserves or replacement alternatives when appropriate. Combining forms and their component machines are not automatically independent simultaneous combatants.
4. **Give each stable a purchasing personality.** Differentiate roster growth, equipment priorities, refinement, Build_Focus, and event preferences using the existing strategy engine. Source-story power levels do not set game balance.
5. **Keep source flavour separate from mechanics.** Flight, transformation, combining, shields, hacking, resurrection, healing, teleportation, unusual anatomy, and signature powers do not automatically add engine abilities or equipment slots.
6. **Select a source edition before production.** Confirm the adaptation, season, machine model, and form. Arena collections may include rivals or characters from different eras; describe those collections as Armoured Souls proposals rather than canonical teams.
7. **Credit fan-created material precisely.** Record the work, creator, and selected continuity. Fan mods, fan games, and fan stories are distinct from their parent franchises' official stories. Original web fiction is labelled separately; shared characters across unrelated fan interpretations do not establish one common canon.
8. **Identify invented embodiments.** A software avatar, ship intelligence, or immobile core does not establish a canonical mobile arena robot. State exactly which body, locomotion, and equipment details are source-based and which are original adaptations. Resolve pending body choices before image production.

**Fit labels:** **Direct** means an armed or combat-machine theme; **Adapt** means utility, civilian, companion, or comic robots need an arena interpretation. Piloted mecha follow the Jaeger and Gundam precedent; biomechanical characters and cyborgs are identified where relevant.

Titanfall/Apex is one shared setting for coverage purposes. Pluto overlaps Astro Boy, Robotech overlaps Macross material, and MechWarrior/MechAssault belong under the existing BattleTech family. They should not inflate the count of untouched franchise omissions.

**Purchasing behaviour**

1. Open with slots 1–3 fully equipped at stage 0 and Roster Expansion L2. Each opening loadout fits the base five-item storage capacity. The opening budget must cover the frames, listed weapons, starting facilities, and the existing maintenance reserve.
2. Apply the current repair and maintenance affordability checks before discretionary purchases. Buy Storage Facility L1 before recruiting slot 4; its ten-item capacity covers the final equipped inventory of every Squadron here, though stored replacement weapons can require more.
3. Buy Roster Expansion L3 and recruit slot 4 with its complete stage-0 loadout. Register the second fixed pair immediately.
4. Prepare Roster Expansion L4 for slot 5 and L5 for slot 6. The supplied catalogue associates L4 with 1,000 prestige and L5 with 3,000 prestige; confirm these against the live game before import.
5. Before buying slot 5, fund both remaining frames, both stage-0 loadouts, the required capacity, and the maintenance reserve. Both final capacity gates must be available. Buy slot 5 and then slot 6 on the next eligible purchase step; do not strand slot 5 indefinitely because the second purchase was unfunded.
6. Register the third pair and second trio when slot 6 joins. Keep the same five team definitions across later equipment upgrades. There is no automatic reassignment or roster growth beyond six.
7. Raise Booking Office to L2 at the completed-roster stage if not already there. Five event slots per robot can accommodate the five preferred team event types, subject to the existing whole-team eligibility rule.
8. Upgrade weapons in the defined 1,4,2,5,3,6 order. A stage change equips the complete new main/offhand loadout. Retain starters still needed by an unbought blueprint; otherwise use the existing storage and resale policy. Increase storage when the actual inventory requires it. All six must reach stage 1 before refinement purchases begin.
9. Buy Workshop levels in the L1 → L3 → L5 → L8 sequence, as the next refinement phase requires them. Execute Hone passes, then Augment passes, then one Sharpen pass, and finally one Forge pass, using the shared refinement rules. Apply the same repair, maintenance, and affordability checks before each facility or refinement purchase.

**Stable event order, shared by all eight:** league_3v3 → league_2v2 → tag_team → tournament_3v3 → tournament_2v2 → league_1v1 → koth → grand_melee → tournament_1v1.

Every event type appears exactly once. Allocation follows the existing rule of 3 + Booking Office level slots per robot and requires every team member to have a free slot. Each robot belongs to one fixed pair and one fixed trio; membership in both formats does not create additional robots. The three pairs are disjoint, and the two trios are disjoint.

### Art rules

Follow the established [Armoured Souls design guide](../sources/DESIGN_SYSTEM_AND_UX_GUIDE.md) and the later catalogue art decisions: square, readable full-body portraits; three-quarter views; dark charcoal industrial backgrounds; realistic materials; and consistent upper-right rim light. Export app assets as **512×512 WebP** while retaining original production masters.

Preserve source proportions, colours, faces, construction, and signature equipment. Human-looking androids and cyborgs retain their authentic appearance; realistic materials should preserve recognizable cartoon and anime geometry. Use consistent camera distance for comparable chassis, controlled internal lights, readable weapons, and clear separation from the background. Frame small companions and giant mecha deliberately for thumbnail recognition.

Artwork exceptions recorded for individual earlier slots remain specific to those slots. In particular, the existing Kamino Clone Legion's original-robot exception does not extend to the source-faithful Star Wars characters proposed here. The numbered original Armoured Souls manufacturers use their own mechanical designs.

The useful production families remain military machinery; industrial and repaired machines; sleek synthetic frames; mechanical creatures; retro icons; and manufactured armies. These group materials and lighting work, without adding stables or reducing distinct source machines to palette variants.
