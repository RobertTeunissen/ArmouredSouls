# Armoured Souls — Automaton Stable Expansion

**Status:** concept proposal for discussion  
**Date:** 8 October 2026  
**Scope:** 89 additional stables, numbered 12–100, to accompany the 11 starters.  
**Blueprint coverage:** 326 slotted robots + 7 fully specified alternatives.  
**Updated:** 9 October 2026. Every proposed robot now has a [complete blueprint](AUTOMATON_ROBOT_BLUEPRINTS.md).

The starter catalogue establishes the direction: recognizable robot properties, characterful stable names, and the six existing strategies. This proposal follows that direction. Stable names, strategy assignments, upgrade personalities, team compositions, and visual treatments below are design proposals rather than claims about the source stories.

The roster column below now lists the exact robot slots and alternatives supplied in the companion blueprint catalogue. Generic machine classes have explicit named units. All reserves and alternatives have complete combat, equipment, refinement, and art blueprints. Some rosters deliberately assemble characters who are rivals in their original story. These are themed arena collections, not necessarily canonical teams.

## How I would choose the additions

1. **Recognizable machines first.** A good portrait should identify the theme before the player reads its name.
2. **One stable per new property.** Keep sequels and subfactions inside that stable. The existing Autobot/Decepticon split remains the starter catalogue's exception. Titanfall and Apex are treated as one shared setting here, with only Titanfall added.
3. **Match the strategy to the available roster.** Iconic lone machines naturally suit Paragon; established pairs suit Duo; mass-produced armies suit Swarm.
4. **Give similar strategies different personalities.** Two Swarm stables can emphasize different ranges, stances, and Build_Focus combinations. Two Paragons can invest in a heavy melee fighter and a precise ranged fighter respectively.
5. **Separate source flavour from engine abilities.** Flight, transformation, hacking, resurrection, healing, teleportation, and unusual anatomy can inform the art and build choices. They do not imply new combat mechanics.

**Fit labels:** **Direct** = an obvious armed/combat machine theme; **Adapt** = a utility robot, small companion, civilian android, or comic character that needs an arena interpretation. Mecha are included, following the Jaeger and Gundam starters. Biomechanical characters are called out where relevant.

Blueprint targets use the newer starter catalogue: Paragon 1, Duo 2, Trio 3, Generalist 3, Tycoon 3, and Swarm 11. Opening counts, reserve slots, fixed team names, event orders, equipment paths, and refinement requirements are specified for all 89 stables in the companion. Where the first concept list exceeded a strategy's supported roster, complete replacement alternatives retain every proposed character.

## Give the automation a recognizable personality

The themes should appear in purchasing and upgrade priorities, using the existing strategy engine. Example design directions:

- **Geth:** prioritize roster growth, then equip each machine for a clear role. Distinguish the stable through the mix of Build_Focus values rather than inventing a network-buff ability.
- **Cylons:** repeat standardized ranged builds, emphasize durable armour, and use numbered units. Their sameness is part of their identity.
- **Aperture:** keep one Warden-style anchor and one Sprinter-style partner. Spend on their complementary equipment rather than adding unrelated characters.
- **Axiom:** WALL-E carries the rugged defensive identity; EVE supplies the accurate ranged identity. Their equipment can improve without erasing their visual contrast.
- **Iron Giant:** put the budget into one Bruiser/Warden-like heavyweight, emphasizing a coherent final weapon and refinement plan.
- **Robot City:** use the Tycoon strategy's facility-led growth. Patched starter machinery gradually gives way to better equipment while retaining its repaired look.
- **Horizon:** pair recognizable machine silhouettes with different focuses: Watcher as scout, Ravager as ranged combatant, Thunderjaw as heavy platform.
- **YoRHa:** use the Trio strategy for the three main androids, with differentiated melee and tactical builds. Hacking and aerial movement remain source flavour unless separately implemented.

## Shared art direction

Follow the existing [Armoured Souls design guide](../sources/DESIGN_SYSTEM_AND_UX_GUIDE.md): 512×512 WebP masters, three-quarter views, dark backgrounds, industrial hard-surface materials, and consistent rim lighting from the top-right. Each robot still needs its own recognizable silhouette and accent colours.

Use the same camera distance convention for comparable chassis, controlled internal lights, readable weapons, and clear separation between the robot and the background. Small utility robots can receive deliberate arena adaptations; giant mecha should be framed for recognition rather than depicted at incompatible physical scales inside the UI.

The most useful visual families for production are:

- **Military machinery:** Titans, Wanzers, BattleMechs, Centurions, NS-5s.
- **Industrial and repaired machines:** WALL-E, Johnny 5, Rodney, SteamWorld robots, Fallout robots.
- **Sleek synthetic frames:** YoRHa, CyberLife, Soong androids, Baymax.
- **Mechanical creatures:** Horizon machines, Zoids, Voltron Lions, Tachikomas.
- **Retro icons:** Iron Giant, Robby, Maschinenmensch, Big O.
- **Manufactured armies:** Geth, Necrons, Badniks, Ultron Sentries, Nindroids.

These are production groupings, not additional stables. They help keep materials and lighting consistent while avoiding portraits that all look like recoloured humanoids.
