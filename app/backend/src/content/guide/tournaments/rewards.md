## Overview

Tournaments offer rewards that go beyond what regular league Battles provide. Every fought round you win earns credits, prestige, and fame — and the deeper you go, the bigger those rewards get. Win the whole thing and you earn a **championship title** that marks your robot as a proven champion.

A bye is different from a fought round: it is a no-simulation walkover. A generated bye shows an expected credit amount in **Upcoming Matches**, but it is not paid and does not appear in **Recent Battles** until the relevant tournament round is processed. At processing, it awards the existing loss-equivalent credit amount once, with no prestige, fame, or streaming income.

The reward structure is designed to make every fought round meaningful. Even a first-round loss earns something, and a bye protects the robot from an early elimination while still paying its defined bye credit reward after processing.

Tournament rewards are independent of your robot's league tier. Credits scale with how many robots entered and how far you've progressed through the bracket. Prestige depends only on how close to the final the round you won is.

## Reward Types

Tournament participation earns four types of rewards:

### Credits (₡)

Cash rewards for each tournament battle won. These start from a base of ₡20,000 and scale up based on tournament size and round progression. The further you advance in a larger tournament, the more credits each win is worth. A bye does not receive a win reward; after its round is processed, it receives the same credit amount a loss would earn for that round.

### Prestige

[Prestige](/guide/prestige-fame/prestige-ranks) is your stable's long-term reputation score. Each round you win earns your stable prestige, counted back from the final. The same table applies to 1v1, 2v2 and 3v3 tournaments, whatever the bracket size, and a team win pays its stable once:

| Round won | Prestige (winning stable) |
|-----------|---------------------------|
| Final | 250 |
| Semi-final | 150 |
| Quarter-final | 90 |
| Round of 16 | 55 |
| Round of 32 | 35 |
| Round of 64 | 20 |
| Round of 128 | 10 |
| Any earlier round | 5 |

A winning run through a 128-robot bracket (7 rounds) earns 610 prestige. Bye events award no prestige.

### Fame

[Fame](/guide/prestige-fame/fame-tiers) is your robot's individual reputation. Tournament fame is driven by exclusivity — the fewer robots remaining when you win, the more fame you earn. Performance bonuses also apply to fame. Bye events award no fame.

| Performance | Bonus | Condition |
|------------|-------|-----------|
| Perfect Victory | 2.0× | Win with 100% HP remaining |
| Dominating | 1.5× | Win with more than 80% HP remaining |
| Comeback | 1.25× | Win with less than 20% HP remaining |
| Normal | 1.0× | Everything else |

```callout-info
Performance bonuses affect fame only, not credits or prestige. Fame rewards exclusivity and how convincingly you win, while credits and prestige reward progression through the bracket. A bye awards none of these fight-based indicators except its defined credit amount after processing.
```

### Championship Title

The tournament winner earns a **championship title** — a distinction attached to your robot's record for the season. Championship titles are the most prestigious achievement in Armoured Souls and cannot be earned through any other means.

```callout-tip
Championship titles accumulate across a season. A robot that wins multiple tournaments collects multiple titles, visible on its profile as proof of tournament dominance. Title counts reset at the season boundary along with the robots that earned them, and each season's totals — overall and per tournament type — are recorded permanently in your season archive.
```

## How Rewards Scale

Tournament rewards are calculated using two multipliers that combine together:

### Tournament Size Multiplier

Larger tournaments mean tougher competition and bigger rewards. The size multiplier uses a logarithmic formula that scales conservatively:

| Participants | Size Multiplier |
|-------------|----------------|
| ~130 | 1.56× |
| ~250 | 1.70× |
| ~500 | 1.85× |
| ~1,000 | 2.00× |
| ~10,000 | 2.50× |
| ~100,000 | 3.00× |

### Round Progression Multiplier

Each round is worth more than the last. The progression multiplier equals your current round divided by the total number of rounds:

For a typical 7-round tournament (128 participants):

| Round | Progression Multiplier |
|-------|----------------------|
| Round 1 (of 7) | 0.14× |
| Round 2 (of 7) | 0.29× |
| Round 3 (of 7) | 0.43× |
| Round 4 (of 7) | 0.57× |
| Round 5 (of 7) | 0.71× |
| Round 6 (of 7) | 0.86× |
| Round 7 — Final | 1.00× |

Early rounds earn modest rewards. The real payoff comes in the later rounds as the progression multiplier approaches 1.0×.

### Combined Formula

Credits for a tournament win are calculated as:

**₡20,000 × size multiplier × round progression multiplier**

For example, in a 128-robot tournament (size multiplier ~1.55×):

| Round | Calculation | Credits Earned |
|-------|-----------|---------------|
| Round 1 | ₡20,000 × 1.55 × 0.14 | ₡4,439 |
| Round 4 | ₡20,000 × 1.55 × 0.57 | ₡17,756 |
| Round 7 (Final) | ₡20,000 × 1.55 × 1.00 | ₡31,072 |

A full championship run through all 7 rounds would earn roughly **₡124,000** in credits — plus 610 prestige, fame, and the championship title.

In a much larger tournament with 1,000 participants (10 rounds, size multiplier 2.00×):

| Round | Calculation | Credits Earned |
|-------|-----------|---------------|
| Round 1 | ₡20,000 × 2.00 × 0.10 | ₡4,000 |
| Round 5 | ₡20,000 × 2.00 × 0.50 | ₡20,000 |
| Round 10 (Final) | ₡20,000 × 2.00 × 1.00 | ₡40,000 |

```callout-warning
A bye pays the same credits a loss pays for that round. Before the round is processed, Upcoming Matches shows an expected amount rather than a zero or an already-paid result. After processing, Recent Battles shows the awarded amount. If you receive a first-round bye and then win your next Match, you advance safely and earn the progression reward for that later round, plus the loss-equivalent credits for the bye round.
```

## Championship Rewards

Winning the final earns everything a normal round win provides, at its highest: the full 1.0× credit progression multiplier and the final's 250 prestige. It also earns:

- A **championship title** attached to your robot's record for the season

The championship title includes the tournament identifier, the date, and your robot's name and stable. Championship titles are the most visible mark of tournament success and cannot be earned through any other means.

```callout-tip
Tournament prestige rises steeply in the last rounds: the semi-final and final together pay 400. Early rounds pay little, so the prestige of a tournament is in going deep, not in entering.
```

```callout-info
Until November 2026 the final carried a +500 championship bonus on top of a small round award, and team tournaments used their own curve multiplied by team size. All tournaments now share one table, and the final is the largest step of a smooth curve.
```

## ELO Impact

Tournament Battles **do** affect your robot's ELO rating. Wins and losses are calculated using the standard K=32 formula, the same one used in league Battles. This means a strong tournament run will boost your ELO, which in turn influences your league matchmaking and future tournament seeding. A bye is not a fight and does not simulate an opponent.

This is different from [King of the Hill](/guide/king-of-the-hill/rewards), where ELO is unaffected. The reasoning: tournaments are competitive, bracket-seeded events where results should carry weight. KotH is a more casual, free-for-all format where ELO is used only for matchmaking group balancing.

```callout-info
Since tournament seeding is based on ELO, doing well in tournaments creates a positive feedback loop — higher ELO means better seeding in the next tournament, which can mean easier early-round matchups and more byes.
```

## Losing Rewards

Losing a tournament Battle still earns a participation reward — **30% of what the winner earns** for that round. Tournament losses don't penalize you beyond the elimination itself.

This means there's no downside to entering tournaments. Even a first-round loss earns something, and the potential upside of a deep run far outweighs the modest participation reward. A bye is not a loss, but its defined credit floor is intentionally the same as the loss reward for that round.

```callout-tip
Always enter tournaments when eligible. The worst case is a first-round loss with a small participation reward or a bye with its loss-equivalent credit reward. The best case is a championship run with scaled credits, 250 prestige for the final alone, fame, and a title. The risk-reward ratio heavily favors participation.
```

## Fame and Exclusivity

Tournament fame works differently from credits and prestige. Instead of scaling with round progression, fame scales with **exclusivity** — how few robots remain when you win. The fewer survivors, the more impressive your victory.

| Robots Remaining | Exclusivity Multiplier |
|-----------------|----------------------|
| 50% of field | 1.41× |
| 25% of field | 2.00× |
| 10% of field | 3.16× |
| Final 2 | Highest multiplier |

This means a semi-final win (with very few robots left) generates far more fame than a first-round win, even without a separate round multiplier. Combined with performance bonuses for dominant or perfect victories, late-round wins can produce significant fame spikes.
